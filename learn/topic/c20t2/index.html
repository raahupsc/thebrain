Brain training and ten ways to think smarter

pp. 93-96
What it is

The brain-training evidence and the book's ten practical tips, with expert commentary from the University of Sheffield explaining that brain training might not be for everyone.

Why it matters

Separates the hype from what actually works.

Where it happens

Whole-brain habits: sleep, exercise, diet, learning and social engagement.

The puzzle

Do puzzle apps make you smarter? Mostly no; lifestyle and real learning do more.

The idea

A neuroscientist at the University of Sheffield explains some of the evidence behind brain training and how it might not be for everyone (pp. 95-96): practising a task makes you better at that task, but the gains rarely transfer broadly to general intelligence. The developmental evidence also shows the age effect: both fluid and crystallised measures grow through life but follow different curves (p. 96). The book's own list of tips (pp. 93-95) is more grounded: sleep well, since consolidation and attention both depend on it; exercise, which supports blood flow and mood; keep learning genuinely new things, which is real training for real circuits; stay socially engaged; manage stress; and feed the brain adequately. The honest summary: there is no shortcut; the boring fundamentals, sleep, exercise, learning, stress control, outperform puzzle apps.

How it works

Task practice yields near transfer only. Lifestyle factors act on the brain's substrate: sleep consolidates, exercise supports vasculature and mood, novel learning drives plasticity (see c09t2, c16).

Key components

  • Near vs far transfer: the brain-training crux
  • Sleep, exercise, diet: the fundamentals
  • Novel learning: real circuit training
  • Stress management: protecting the machinery

Examples

The sudoku plateau

Months of daily sudoku make you excellent at sudoku, not dramatically smarter overall.

Mechanism: Near transfer without far transfer.

Learning an instrument at 40

Slower than a child, but measurable brain changes still accrue: adults can rewire with effort (see c09t2).

Mechanism: Adult plasticity through sustained novel practice.

Connects to

  • Plasticity (c09t2) is the mechanism; sleep (c11) and learning (c16) are the levers.

Takeaways

  • Brain-training gains rarely transfer beyond the trained task (pp. 95-96).
  • Sheffield's expert: brain training might not be for everyone (p. 95).
  • The fundamentals, sleep, exercise, novel learning and stress control, beat apps.
  • Adults can still rewire with sustained effort.
