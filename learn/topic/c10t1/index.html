The ten facts tour

pp. 38-45
What it is

A list of ten surprising brain phenomena: earworms, yawning, phantom vibrations and more.

Why it matters

Weird facts make memorable hooks for the underlying science.

Where it happens

Spans memory, perception, sleep and social cognition.

The puzzle

Everyday oddities that deserve explanation.

The idea

Among the ten: earworms, the songs that loop in your head, which involve auditory memory replaying a short loop; why we yawn and yawns spreading socially; phantom vibrations, feeling your phone buzz when it did not; and others spanning sleep, dreams and perception (pp. 38-45). Each fact points at a deeper mechanism: earworms show that loops of memory can self-trigger, helped along by repetition and music's predictability; contagious yawning suggests social mirroring in the brain; phantom vibrations show expectation-driven perception, where anticipation generates sensation. The book's trick here is teaching mechanism through curiosity: every oddity maps onto a system covered elsewhere in the book.

How it works

Varies by fact: memory loops self-reactivate (earworms), the social brain mirrors others' behaviour (contagious yawns), expectation biases perception (phantom buzzes).

Key components

  • Earworm: a self-looping auditory memory
  • Contagious yawning: socially triggered mirroring
  • Phantom perception: expectation generating sensation

Examples

The stuck song

A catchy chorus returns all afternoon: a short auditory loop keeps re-firing, strengthened by repetition.

Mechanism: Self-triggering auditory memory loop.

Feeling a buzz that never came

You reach for a phone that did not vibrate: your brain predicted the sensation and delivered it.

Mechanism: Expectation-driven perception.

Connects to

  • Earworms ↔ memory (c16); phantom buzzes ↔ prediction in vision (c07, c18); yawns ↔ social brain (c13).

Takeaways

  • Earworms are self-looping auditory memories (pp. 38-45).
  • Phantom sensations show perception is expectation-laden.
  • Each weird fact maps onto a real mechanism from the book's chapters.
