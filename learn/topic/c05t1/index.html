The pain pathway and types of pain

pp. 21-24
What it is

The route pain signals take from injured tissue to conscious experience, and the categories pain falls into.

Why it matters

Understanding the pathway explains why pain can be blocked at different points.

Where it happens

From receptors in skin and tissue, up spinal tracts, to the brain's pain-processing regions.

The puzzle

Pain protects us, but it can malfunction and persist long after injury heals.

The idea

Pain begins with specialised receptors called nociceptors, which respond to damaging mechanical, thermal or chemical stimuli. Their signals travel along sensory neurons into the spinal cord, cross to the opposite side, and ascend to the brain, where regions including the thalamus and cortex make the signal conscious and locate it in the body. The book distinguishes types of pain (pp. 23-24): acute pain, the useful alarm of immediate injury; chronic pain, which persists beyond normal healing; and neuropathic pain, caused by damaged nerves themselves firing without injury. The section opens with the striking idea that pain is generated by the brain as an experience, not simply passively received: the same injury can hurt differently depending on context, attention and expectation. That is why distraction, fear and even mood change pain intensity (pp. 22-24).

How it works

Injury → nociceptors fire → signal ascends the spinal cord via dedicated tracts → thalamus relays → cortex interprets and localises → emotional regions assign urgency. Descending pathways from the brain can dial the signal up or down at the spinal relay, the biological basis of context-dependent pain.

Key components

  • Nociceptors: danger-detecting receptors
  • Spinal tracts: the ascending pain cables
  • Thalamus: relay station
  • Cortex: conscious, located pain
  • Descending modulation: brain-controlled volume knob

Examples

Not noticing a cut during a match

Athletes often discover wounds only after the game: attention and adrenaline suppressed the pain signal's ascent.

Mechanism: Descending modulation reduced spinal relay transmission.

Sunburn tenderness

After sunburn, light touch hurts because damaged tissue sensitises the receptors, lowering their threshold.

Mechanism: Peripheral sensitisation of nociceptors.

Connects to

  • Shares spinal relay design with the reflex arc (c03t3); gate control theory is the Lab's pain simulation (c05t2); chronic pain returns in the disorders thread.

Takeaways

  • Pain signals run: nociceptors → spinal cord → thalamus → cortex (pp. 22-24).
  • Acute pain protects; chronic pain is a malfunctioning alarm (pp. 23-24).
  • Neuropathic pain arises from damaged nerves firing spontaneously.
  • The brain modulates pain, which is why context changes intensity.
