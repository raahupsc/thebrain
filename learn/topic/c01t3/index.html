Nerves, the spinal cord and gray vs white matter

p. 8
What it is

Nerves are the body's transmission cables; the spinal cord is structurally part of the brain, with its gray and white matter arranged inside-out compared to the brain.

Why it matters

Explains how signals physically travel between brain and toes, and a classic exam-style fact about tissue arrangement.

Where it happens

Nerves run through the whole body; the spinal cord runs inside the backbone from the brain stem downward.

The puzzle

How does a signal get from your toenail up to your brain? Nerves carry it the whole way.

The idea

'Nerves are the transmission cables that carry brain waves in the human body,' says Sol Diamond, an assistant professor at the Thayer School of Engineering at Dartmouth (p. 8). Nerves communicate these signals from one point to another, whether from your toenail up to your brain or from the side of your head. Scientists have known for the past 100 years or so that the spinal cord is actually part of the brain (p. 8). The difference is arrangement: the brain has gray matter on the outside, protected by the skull, with white matter protected on the inside. The spinal cord is reversed, with gray matter inside and white matter outside. Gray matter is dense with neuron cell bodies doing the processing; white matter is the insulated cabling that carries signals long distances.

How it works

A signal generated at a fingertip enters a peripheral nerve, ascends the spinal cord's white-matter tracts, relays through the brain stem and reaches the cortex. Motor commands descend the same cables in the opposite direction.

Key components

  • Nerves: transmission cables carrying signals between body and brain
  • Gray matter: neuron cell bodies, the processing layer
  • White matter: insulated fibre tracts, the cabling layer
  • Spinal cord: part of the brain, gray inside / white outside

Examples

Toenail to brain

You stub your toe: the signal runs up a nerve, through the spinal cord, to the brain, where it becomes pain. Diamond's toenail example is literally this route (p. 8).

Mechanism: Long peripheral nerves plus spinal tracts carry the signal the full distance.

Inside-out wiring

Think of the spinal cord as the brain's cable rolled up backwards: processing core in the middle, cabling wrapped around the outside.

Mechanism: Reversed gray/white arrangement accommodates tracts running up and down.

Connects to

  • Sets up the reflex arc (c03t3): fastest signals never wait for the cortex.
  • Links to pain pathway structure in c05.

Takeaways

  • Nerves are the transmission cables carrying signals in the human body (Sol Diamond, p. 8).
  • The spinal cord is structurally part of the brain, a fact known for about 100 years (p. 8).
  • Brain: gray matter outside, white matter inside. Spinal cord: the reverse.
  • Gray matter processes; white matter transmits.
