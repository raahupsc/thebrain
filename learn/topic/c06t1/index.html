The blood-brain barrier

p. 28
What it is

A selective border of cells lining the brain's blood vessels that strictly controls what enters brain tissue.

Why it matters

The brain needs stable chemistry and protection from toxins and pathogens.

Where it happens

Capillaries throughout the brain.

The puzzle

Blood carries nutrients, but also wastes, toxins and immune cells that could harm neurons.

The idea

The blood-brain barrier (BBB) is formed by tightly joined cells lining the brain's capillaries. Unlike vessels elsewhere in the body, which leak freely, brain capillaries admit only what the brain needs: oxygen, glucose and small lipid-soluble molecules, while blocking most large molecules, toxins and many drugs. This selectivity is why some medicines that work elsewhere in the body cannot reach the brain: drug designers must either use molecules that cross the barrier or deliver them directly. The barrier also protects the brain from infection, but at a cost: immune cells largely cannot enter, so brain infections are serious and hard to treat. The book positions the BBB within its anatomy tour of the brain's defences (p. 28).

How it works

Endothelial cells in brain capillaries are joined by tight junctions, sealing the gaps. Transport systems ferry permitted molecules across; everything else must be small and fat-soluble, or carried by a specific transporter, to enter.

Key components

  • Tight junctions: the seals between capillary cells
  • Transporters: gatekeepers for glucose and amino acids
  • Lipid solubility: the property that lets some drugs cross

Examples

Why some cold medicines don't touch the brain

A decongestant acts on body vessels but not brain ones, because its molecules cannot cross the BBB.

Mechanism: Size and charge exclude the drug from brain tissue.

Caffeine gets through

Caffeine affects alertness quickly because it is small and fat-soluble, crossing the barrier easily.

Mechanism: Lipid-soluble molecules pass the capillary seals.

Connects to

  • Explains why treating brain disorders (Parkinson's, c03t3) is hard; drug molecules also matter in the mood chemistry of c13.

Takeaways

  • The BBB is a selective border of tightly joined capillary cells (p. 28).
  • It admits oxygen, glucose and small fat-soluble molecules; blocks most toxins, large molecules and many drugs.
  • Brain infections are serious partly because immune access is limited.
  • Drug design must plan for the barrier.
