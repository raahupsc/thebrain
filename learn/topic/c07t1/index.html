From light waves to the visual cortex

pp. 29-32
What it is

The full visual pipeline: light, eye optics, retinal cells, optic nerves and cortical processing.

Why it matters

Vision is the brain's dominant sense and a model for all sensory processing.

Where it happens

From the outside world through the eyes to the occipital cortex at the brain's back.

The puzzle

The eyes do not produce images; they simply take in light waves and pass on information (p. 30).

The idea

Light reflects off objects and enters the eye through the pupil, where it is refracted by the lens and cornea so rays reach the retina at the back of the eye (p. 29). The retina is a sensory membrane of millions of light-sensitive cells: rods for dim light, cones for colour and detail. When light waves hit the retina they activate certain cells, which fire electrical signals down the optic nerves. The nerves meet and cross at the optic chiasm, so signals from each eye's left visual field go to the brain's right side and vice versa (p. 30). No images are formed within the eyes themselves; the brain assembles the picture: visual areas in the occipital cortex process edges, motion, colour and form, and higher regions recognise objects and faces (c08). The section closes with how animal eyes differ, from colour ranges to night vision (pp. 31-32).

How it works

Light → cornea and lens focus → retina rods/cones convert to electrical signals → optic nerves → optic chiasm crossing → occipital visual cortex processes features → higher areas interpret meaning.

Key components

  • Cornea and lens: focus light rays (p. 29)
  • Retina: light-sensitive membrane of rods and cones (p. 29)
  • Optic nerve: cable to the brain
  • Optic chiasm: crossing point sorting left/right fields (p. 30)
  • Occipital cortex: feature processing and image assembly

Examples

Reading this page

Light from the screen focuses on your retina, rod and cone cells fire, and cortical areas assemble letters into words within a fraction of a second.

Mechanism: The full visual pipeline in action.

Walking from sunlight into a dark cinema

For minutes everything is black, then details emerge as dim-light rods take over from cones.

Mechanism: Photoreceptor switching between cone and rod vision.

Connects to

  • Face recognition continues in c08; illusions in c18 show the interpretation layer misfiring; sleep dreams reuse visual imagery (c11).

Takeaways

  • The eyes take in light waves; images are built in the brain (p. 30).
  • Cornea and lens refract light onto the retina (p. 29).
  • Rods handle dim light; cones handle colour and detail.
  • The optic chiasm crosses signals so each brain half handles the opposite visual field (p. 30).
